# References

Primary sources for the claims in these docs. Effect sizes quoted are from the
source's own tables/abstracts unless marked otherwise.

## Testing effect / retrieval practice — meta-analyses

**Rowland, C. A. (2014).** The effect of testing versus restudy on retention: A
meta-analytic review of the testing effect. *Psychological Bulletin, 140*(6),
1432–1463. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037559
- g = 0.50 [0.42, 0.58] across 61 experiments / 159 effect sizes.
- Feedback g = 0.73 vs no feedback 0.39; retention ≥1 day g = 0.69 vs <1 day 0.41;
  cued recall 0.61 > prose 0.58 > paired associates 0.59 > single words 0.39 >
  recognition 0.29.

**Adesope, O. O., Trevisan, D. A., & Sundararajan, N. (2017).** Rethinking the
use of tests: A meta-analysis of practice testing. *Review of Educational
Research, 87*(3), 659–701. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654316689306
- g = 0.61 fixed-effects [0.58, 0.65] / 0.70 random-effects; 272 effect sizes,
  N = 15,427.
- vs restudying g = 0.51; vs filler/no activity 0.93; vs mixture 0.71.
- MCQ practice g = 0.70 vs short answer 0.48 (contrasts with classroom orderings —
  see 01 §4.3).
- Gap <1 day g = 0.56 vs 1–6 days g = 0.82.

**Yang, C., Luo, L., Vadillo, M. A., Yu, R., & Shanks, D. R. (2021).** Testing
(quizzing) boosts classroom learning: A systematic and meta-analytic review.
*Psychological Bulletin, 147*(4), 399–435. https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000309
- g = 0.499; 222 studies, 573 effects, N = 48,478.
- vs no/filler 0.610; vs fewer questions 0.465; vs restudying 0.330; vs other
  elaborative strategies 0.095.
- Format: fill-in-the-blank 0.773, short answer 0.638, MCQ 0.567, free recall 0.238.
- Matched format 0.531 vs mismatched 0.399; corrective feedback 0.537 vs 0.374;
  post-class 0.536 vs pre-class 0.186; semester-long >0.5 vs single class 0.385;
  untested material 0.321.
- Companion analysis: quizzing reduces test anxiety, g ≈ −0.52 (25 studies).

**Pan, S. C., & Rickard, T. C. (2018).** Transfer of test-enhanced learning:
Meta-analytic review and synthesis. *Psychological Bulletin, 144*(7), 710–756.
https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000151
- d = 0.40 [0.31, 0.50]; 192 effects, 122 experiments, N = 10,382.
- Response congruency: no congruency d = 0.28, congruent d ≈ 0.58 (+0.30).
- Elaborated retrieval practice: +0.23. Initial accuracy: +0.0058 d per +1 pp.
- Greatest transfer to application/inference and across formats; weakest to
  rearranged items, untested material, worked examples.
- PET-PEESE correction substantially reduced the intercept — i.e. the baseline
  transfer claim is fragile without those moderators.

## Core experiments and theory

**Roediger, H. L., III, & Karpicke, J. D. (2006).** Test-enhanced learning:
Taking memory tests improves long-term retention. *Psychological Science, 17*(3),
249–255. — The study/restudy vs study/test comparison; re-reading wins at 5 min,
testing wins at 1 week; learners' predictions favour re-reading.

**Karpicke, J. D., Butler, A. C., & Roediger, H. L., III (2009).** Metacognitive
strategies in student learning: Do students practise retrieval when they study on
their own? *Memory, 17*(4), 471–479. — Students overwhelmingly choose re-reading.

**Roediger, H. L., III, & Marsh, E. J. (2005).** The positive and negative
consequences of multiple-choice testing. *Journal of Experimental Psychology:
Learning, Memory, and Cognition, 31*(5), 1155–1159. — Negative suggestion effect;
plausible lures can later be endorsed as true.

**Halamish, V., & Bjork, R. A. (2011).** When does testing enhance retention? A
distribution-based interpretation of retrieval as a memory modifier. *JEP:LMC,
37*(4), 801–812. — Bifurcation model.

**Kornell, N., Bjork, R. A., & Garcia, M. A. (2011).** Why tests appear to
prevent forgetting: A distribution-based interpretation. *Journal of Memory and
Language, 64*(2), 79–88. — Bifurcation model, second source.

**Glaser, J., & Richter, T. (2018).** Examining the testing effect in university
teaching: Retrievability and question format matter. *Frontiers in Psychology, 9*,
2412. — Testing effect present at 46–81% retrievability; stable over 23 weeks.
See also Glaser & Richter (2023, 2025) for lecture-hall replications
(Cohen's d = 0.55).

**Bjork, R. A., & Bjork, E. L. (2011).** Making things hard on yourself, but in a
good way: Creating desirable difficulties to enhance learning. — The desirable
difficulty framing.

**Cepeda, N. J., Pashler, H., Vul, E., Wixted, J. T., & Rohrer, D. (2006).**
Distributed practice in verbal recall tasks: A review and quantitative synthesis.
*Psychological Bulletin, 132*(3), 354–380. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354
- 839 assessments, 317 experiments, 184 articles. Optimal inter-study interval
  increases with retention interval.

## Item writing / measurement

**Rodriguez, M. C. (2005).** Three options are optimal for multiple-choice items:
A meta-analysis of 80 years of research. *Educational Measurement: Issues and
Practice, 24*(2), 3–13. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-3992.2005.00006.x
- 56 independent studies; 3-option items match 4–5 option items on discrimination
  and reliability; most extra options are non-functional (<5% selection).

**Haladyna, T. M., Downing, S. M., & Rodriguez, M. C. (2002).** A review of
multiple-choice item-writing guidelines for classroom assessment. *Applied
Measurement in Education, 15*(3), 309–333. — The item-writing guideline taxonomy
behind DR-11.

**Haladyna, T. M., & Rodriguez, M. C. (2013).** *Developing and validating test
items.* Routledge. — Book-length treatment; source of the 3-option argument in
textbook form.

## Scheduling

**Ye, J., Su, J., & Cao, Y. (2022).** A stochastic shortest path algorithm for
optimizing spaced repetition scheduling. *KDD '22*, 4381–4390.
https://doi.org/10.1145/3534678.3539081
- The DHP/DSR model and SSP-MMC scheduler behind FSRS; 220M review logs;
  deployed in MaiMemo; 12.6% improvement over prior state of the art.

**Su, J., Ye, J., Nie, L., Cao, Y., & Chen, Y. (2023).** Optimizing spaced
repetition schedule by capturing the dynamics of memory. *IEEE TKDE.*
https://doi.org/10.1109/TKDE.2023.3251721 — Follow-up work; time-series memory
dataset.

**Open Spaced Repetition.** *FSRS algorithm documentation* (v4–v7 formulas and
default parameters), github.com/open-spaced-repetition/awesome-fsrs/wiki.
- DSR state (S, D, R); reviews later in the forgetting curve yield larger
  stability gains; stability growth saturates; defaults validated on
  10k–20k-user datasets.

## Automatic question generation (for the pipeline design)

**Kurdi, G., Leo, J., Parsia, B., Sattler, U., & Al-Emari, S. (2020).** A
systematic review of automatic question generation for educational purposes.
*International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education, 30*, 121–204.
https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-019-00186-y — 93 studies; the recurring finding is
inconsistent evaluation, which is why 03 §6 defines a machine-checkable gate.

**Ch, D. R., & Saha, S. K. (2020).** Automatic multiple choice question
generation from text: A survey. *IEEE Transactions on Learning Technologies,
13*(1), 14–25. — Generic MCQ-generation workflow; distractor generation is the
weak link.

**Alhazmi, E., Sheng, Q. Z., Zhang, W. E., Zaib, M., & Alhazmi, A. (2024).**
Distractor generation in multiple-choice tasks: A survey of methods, datasets,
and evaluation. *EMNLP 2024*, 14437–14458. — Confirms distractor quality as the
dominant failure mode; supports DR-10 and the `distractor_pick_rate` metric.

## Notes on citation hygiene

- Effect sizes are transcribed from abstracts/tables, not recomputed. Where two
  syntheses disagree (Adesope vs Yang on MCQ vs short answer), 01 §4.3 says so
  rather than picking a winner.
- Claims tagged `[W]` or `[?]` in the design docs are deliberately not attributed
  to a source; they are engineering judgement.
- No claim here rests on a single unreplicated study without being marked.
